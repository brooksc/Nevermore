Creating an app-specific password for iCloud Mail

Two minutes at account.apple.com, and you only do it once.

Nevermore signs in to iCloud Mail over IMAP with an app-specific password: a separate, revocable password that works for one app and nothing else. Your ordinary account password will not work, and that is deliberate — there is no OAuth consent screen and no third party in the middle. Covers @icloud.com, @me.com, and @mac.com addresses, and any custom domain you have added to iCloud Mail.

Creating one

  1. Sign in to your Apple Account at account.apple.com. Your account must be protected with two-factor authentication.
  2. In the Sign-In and Security section, select App-Specific Passwords.
  3. Select Generate an app-specific password, then follow the steps on screen.
  4. Copy the password Apple shows you.
Apple calls this an app-specific password, never an "app password" — that is the wording to look for on the page. Your ordinary Apple Account password will not sign in over IMAP.

Apple's own instructions →

Then, in Nevermore

Paste it into the App password field on Nevermore's sign-in screen. Spaces are fine — the app strips them. It goes straight into the macOS Keychain, is never written to disk in the clear, and never leaves your Mac.

An app password is a credential: it grants access to your mailbox. Put it in Nevermore's password field, and nowhere else — not into a note, an email to yourself, or a chat window. If you ever want to cut Nevermore off, delete the app-specific password at your provider; nothing else about your account changes.

If sign-in still fails

  • Check you entered the app-specific password, not your normal account password. Providers reject both with the same error, so the message looks identical.
  • Most providers show the password only once and cannot show it again. If you did not copy it, delete that one and create another — they are free and disposable.
  • If you deleted the password at your provider, or changed something about the account, create a new one and re-enter it.
  • Still stuck? Get in touch — include your provider and what the error said.

Checked against Apple's own instructions on 22 August 2026. Providers move these settings without warning; if what you see does not match, their page is the authoritative one and we would be glad to hear about it.
