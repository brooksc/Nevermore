Creating an app password for Yahoo Mail

Two minutes at your Yahoo Account Security page, and you only do it once.

Nevermore signs in to Yahoo Mail over IMAP with an app password: a separate, revocable password that works for one app and nothing else. Your ordinary account password will not work, and that is deliberate — there is no OAuth consent screen and no third party in the middle. Covers @yahoo.com, @ymail.com, and @rocketmail.com addresses.

Creating one

  1. Sign in to your Yahoo Account Security page.
  2. Under External connections, choose Create app password.
  3. Follow the prompts, then copy the password Yahoo generates.
Yahoo decides whether your account is eligible to create one, and support cannot override that. Their advice: use a browser you have signed in to Yahoo with on several days, and not a private or Incognito window. Changing your main Yahoo password does not invalidate an app password — to revoke one you have to delete it.

Yahoo's own instructions →

Then, in Nevermore

Paste it into the App password field on Nevermore's sign-in screen. Spaces are fine — the app strips them. It goes straight into the macOS Keychain, is never written to disk in the clear, and never leaves your Mac.

An app password is a credential: it grants access to your mailbox. Put it in Nevermore's password field, and nowhere else — not into a note, an email to yourself, or a chat window. If you ever want to cut Nevermore off, delete the app password at your provider; nothing else about your account changes.

If sign-in still fails

  • Check you entered the app password, not your normal account password. Providers reject both with the same error, so the message looks identical.
  • Most providers show the password only once and cannot show it again. If you did not copy it, delete that one and create another — they are free and disposable.
  • If you deleted the password at your provider, or changed something about the account, create a new one and re-enter it.
  • Still stuck? Get in touch — include your provider and what the error said.

Checked against Yahoo's own instructions on 22 August 2026. Providers move these settings without warning; if what you see does not match, their page is the authoritative one and we would be glad to hear about it.
